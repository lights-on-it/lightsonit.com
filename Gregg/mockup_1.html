About Gregg Owen

Gregg Owen has lived several lives, and each one set up the next.

He taught himself to play keyboard and, in 1969, joined The American Breed, launching a music career that spanned two decades and put him on stages across the country. Wary of a cutthroat music business, he did something almost no touring musician would: he put himself through law school from the road, determined to protect his band on his own terms.

Then he traded the stage for the courtroom. Gregg joined the Cook County State’s Attorney’s Office, where he was appointed by Richard M. Daley, then State’s Attorney for Cook County, to lead the fight against the city’s street-gang violence as Chief of Gang Prosecutions. The work was high-profile and dangerous; its success brought threats against Gregg and his family. He worked alongside the U.S. Attorney’s Office and officials in Washington, prosecuting cases that defined one of Chicago’s most corrupt eras.

Today he tells those stories from the inside. His true-crime book Convergence, written with Jonathan Dixon, revives a brutal late-1970s Chicago double murder, a case Gregg helped try and one that came perilously close to being swept aside.

The Book

Convergence

By Gregg Owen & Jonathan Dixon · True Crime

A fast-paced true story of a brutal double murder in late-1970s Chicago, a fairly clear-cut case that was almost closed. Two young people stabbed to death. One terrified eyewitness. A defendant with money, power, the right connections, and a formidable defense team. Told from inside the prosecution by Gregg Owen and rendered with the pace of a thriller by co-author Jonathan Dixon, it reads like fiction, except every word of it happened.
